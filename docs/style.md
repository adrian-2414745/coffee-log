
Theme 1: Functionalist Modernism (The Dieter Rams / Braun Look)
Style Essence

This style is rooted in mid-century German industrial design. It operates on the principle that good design is as little design as possible. It is mathematical, intensely orderly, and treats a digital interface like a premium, physical injection-molded plastic product. Everything is quiet, structural, and inherently logical.
1. Color Palette

    Primary Canvas: Matte off-whites, neutral light grays, and soft creams.

    Structure & Text: Deep charcoal or soft graphite (rarely pure, harsh black).

    Functional Accents: Used with extreme restraint (less than 5% of the screen). A single button might be standard calculator yellow, an active indicator might be a soft ruby red, or a power state might be a muted forest green.

2. Typography

    Characteristics: Highly legible, geometric, neutral sans-serif typefaces (e.g., Helvetica Neue, Inter, Akzidenz-Grotesk).

    Hierarchy: Strict, predictable type scales. Text is never decorated—meaning no italics or random underlines. Variations in hierarchy are achieved solely through deliberate weight changes (Regular to Bold) and clean spacing.

3. UI Geometry & Tactility

    Card & Container Design: Elements use flush, flat grids or very subtle, soft-molded inner shadows (inset) to look like physical plastic keys or switches built directly into a housing panel.

    Corners: Tight, precise radiuses (typically between 4px and 8px)—never perfectly sharp corners, but never overly round or bubbly.

    Interactive Elements: Pill-shaped toggle switches, segmented button groups that look like physical mechanical buttons, and perfectly circular dials.

Theme 2: Technical Schematic (The Patent Drawing Look)
Style Essence

This aesthetic mimics the raw, analytical look of an architectural blueprint, patent application, or drafting-table schematic. It strips away all illusion of "software finishes" and instead treats the screen like an active engineering document. It trades polished styling for precise, visible measurement.
1. Color Palette

   The Draft Paper: A textured parchment, manila folder cream, or engineering-pad grid paper background with dark indigo, sepia, or technical black linework.

    Accents: Monochromatic by default. If a second color is used, it mimics a physical markup tool—like a red ink stamp or a faint blueprint highlighter yellow.

2. Typography

    Characteristics: Clean, monospaced fonts (e.g., JetBrains Mono, Roboto Mono) or technical sans-serifs that mimic architectural plotting and lettering stencils.

    Hierarchy: Often uses uppercase lettering for headers and functional metadata labels. Numbers and values are highly emphasized, looking like entries in a data table or dimensions on a blueprint.

3. UI Geometry & Tactility

    Card & Container Design: There are no solid cards or shaded blocks. Instead, spaces are defined entirely by ultra-thin, precise bounding lines (1px width). Bounding boxes often feature structural artifacts like crosshairs at the corners, alignment rules, or slight overshoots where lines intersect.

    Layout Elements: Employs physical drafting details like fine background grids (dot or square matrix layouts), measurement hashmarks along the edges of panels, and leader lines (arrows pointing from a label directly to a specific value).

    Interactive Elements: Perfectly square checkboxes, sharp 90-degree angular borders, and sliders or progress bars that look like linear calibration scales.
